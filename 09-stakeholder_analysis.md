In essence, a stakeholder is any individual who affects the Smart Parking Platform or is affected by the Smart Parking Platform. The following stakeholders have been analyzed using two criteria: power (the extent of the influence on scope, budget, schedule, or acceptance of the project) and interest (the degree of concern about the outcome), each of which can be rated between 1 and 10. See the image stakeholder-grid.png for details.

# Scores
- City (client and sponsor): power 9, interest 9
- Software Corp management: power 8, interest 5
- Parking operators: power 7, interest 9
- Development teams (13 people, 6 teams): power 6, interest 9
- Payment provider: power 7, interest 3
- Compliance and legal reviewers (PCI-DSS, city ordinance): power 8, interest 3
- Drivers (end users): power 3, interest 9
- Mapping service vendor: power 4, interest 2
- City residents and nearby businesses: power 2, interest 3

# The four quadrants
Manage closely (high power, high interest)

- City: Approves the contract and makes payments based on milestones. Monthly presentation to the city, a short report containing the status of the milestones, budget compared to the $488,336 budget, SPI & CPI, and the High risks.
- Parking operators: They use the dashboard on a daily basis and their approval is a schedule risk (R-10). The Product Owner will prioritize their requests and feedback deadlines will be specified in the contract.
- Development teams: They deliver the product and own the schedule. Daily stand-ups, sprint planning, sprint review, and sprint retrospective every 2 weeks.

Stay Satisfied (high power-low interest)

- Software Corporation management: interested in the timely execution of the project and on budget, but do not look into all details. Monthly report including budget, milestones, risks, and escalation for High risks.
- Payment provider: control sandbox access and gateway uptime (R-05 and R-02). Apply for sandbox access in advance and check the status of your gateway.
- Compliance and legal reviewers: capable of delaying the go-live launch due to the non-compliance of payment flow with PCI-DSS regulations (R-12). Design review in Sprint 2 instead of Month 3 legal review.

Stay Informed (low power-high interest)

- Drivers: very interested, yet incapable of influencing decisions. Information received through notifications in an app and release notes, feedback from UAT.

Monitor (low power, low interest)

- Vendor providing mapping services: included in the vendor SLA (R-03). Just check any rate limit restrictions and deprecated services information.
- Local city inhabitants and neighboring businesses: no role. Be aware of any public issues that might be raised to the city.

# What is changed in the plan
- Both the operators and the city belong to the same quadrant, so both receive pre-dashboard/app a priori assessment point.
- Two of the four High level risks (R-05 and R-12) are generated by stakeholders belonging to the Keep satisfied quadrant – payment provider and compliance review board, so the weekly risk review remains a part of the agenda.
- Communication plan presented in Section 4.5 fits the above stakeholders: demonstration to the city monthly; report to the management on a monthly basis and sprint activities for the team.
